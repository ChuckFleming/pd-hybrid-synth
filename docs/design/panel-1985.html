PD Hybrid · direction 02 · finish study

Panel 1985, in six finishes

Same construction throughout — silkscreen legends, a coloured legend stripe under the wordmark, moulded plastic with a fine vertical grain, physical knob caps with a light pointer. Only the finish changes. That is exactly how the real hardware ranged: one tooling, several colourways.

Since you're leaning towards multiple display options, these are drawn as a family that could all ship together rather than as rival one-offs.

Fixed since the last study: the dropdown fields were dark-on-dark and the waveform traces vanished on dark panels. Both came from one token doing two jobs — display wells and select fields shared a colour. They're separate now, so every panel below is legible. Nothing from the first study was discarded; the original ten are still on file.

Notes

  • 2b Graphite is the literal answer to "darker Panel 1985" — nothing changes but the plastic. If you want the pick you already made, just at night, it's this one.
  • 2d Carbon goes furthest and is the only one using two accents with real jobs: red for controls, amber for live values. That's the same split that makes 10 work, which makes the two sit together well as a pair.
  • 2c and 2e are the ones with a point of view. Midnight is warm and forgiving; Lab is cool and clinical. Lab is also the most different from Phosphor, so a set of {Lab, Phosphor, Cream} would cover a lot of ground with three themes.
  • If you ship a themed set, three or four is the sweet spot — one light, one neutral dark, one committed. More than that and they stop feeling curated.
  • Knob caps are worth deciding separately from the panel: dark metal (2a, 2b, 2d, 2e) reads as pro gear, cream (2c, 2f) reads as vintage consumer. It's a bigger tonal lever than it looks.

Tell me which finishes you want to keep, and whether you'd like the themed set to be three or four. Then we can move on to the layout, which is the part that will actually make it easier to use.
