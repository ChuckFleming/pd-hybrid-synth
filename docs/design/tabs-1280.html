What to look at

  • Every page fits, and none is close to the limit. The tallest column across all four is MOD at 347 px of the 592 available. VOICE was the constraint; once it fits, the rest are comfortable.
  • GLOBAL is the odd one out. Five cards, one of which is a single dropdown, leaves the third column almost empty. Three ways to fix it, none of which I've assumed: fold Quality into Tempo as one "Engine" card; drop GLOBAL to two wider columns; or move something onto it — Tuning and Voice Allocation could take bigger displays.
  • SHAPE has 280 px of slack. The filter response and transfer curves are the obvious things to grow into it — they're the displays that actually reward being bigger, and I shrank them to hit the budget. Say the word and they go back up.
  • MOD needed a full-width band, and that is a real finding. My first pass showed the stage-envelope card with three knobs; it actually carries 18 per bank — R1–R8, L1–L8, Amt and Sus Pt — across three banks behind the DCO / DCW / MULTI selector. Sixteen knobs in a row need ~464 px and a column is 415 px, so it cannot live in one. It spans the full width instead, which also lets the curve be 1000 px wide — the eight breakpoints are genuinely easier to grab than they are today.
  • That makes "full-width band" a required feature of the layout engine, not a nicety: a card whose span equals the column count takes its own row. Global EQ on OUT and Routing on SHAPE could use the same treatment if you want them wider.
  • MOD is the tight page — 35 px of clearance with everything expanded. That is the worst case: collapsing NUMERIC drops the 16-knob row and returns another ~63 px. Every figure on this page is measured from the rendered layout, not estimated.
  • Two real bugs came out of measuring rather than estimating. A knob cell is 78 px, not the 61 px I specified — it carries the value readout as well as the caption, so every card was ~17 px per knob row taller than budgeted. And a row height is floored by the tallest knob cell beside it, so shrinking a display does nothing until you shrink the knob next to it. Both apply directly to the C++ metrics.
  • The two LFO cards keep their inline rate knob beside the waveform, which is how they work today — the rate is part of the graphic, not a separate row.
  • Slack is not the same as waste. Empty space below the last card is a page that could hold more, not a page padding itself out. The waste you objected to was between cards, and that's gone.
